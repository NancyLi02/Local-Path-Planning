Step E — centralized local replanning

A safety shield that stays out of the way

Building the specified attention policy for variable-N multi-AMR local replanning, and measuring what the shield around it is actually made of.

step_e_v1/ · 6 AMRs, 2 workers, 420 frames, 5 seeds · 2026-08-25

The Step-E specification asks for one module: take a conflict cluster of up to four rail-following AMRs, build one observation per AMR, run a shared-weight attention policy, decode a bounded (goal_fwd, goal_lat, speed_scale) per AMR, roll each action into a short-horizon trajectory, and let a space-time reservation shield decide which of them may execute.

The package now does exactly that, with two planners sharing every stage except the proposal: V0, TTC-priority sequential, and V1, the learned policy. Because they differ in one place only, every number below is attributable.

The shield checks the worker tube time-aligned, the reserved trajectories of higher-priority AMRs, the inflated workstation rectangles, and the manoeuvre's kinematic feasibility. Safe candidates are ranked by J = w₁Jworker + w₂Jamr + w₃Jroute + w₄Jsmooth + w₅Jdelay. Nothing reaches a wheel without passing it.

Results

Four rungs of one ladder, sharing everything except the action set — same observation, same TTC priority order, same shield, same commands.

Stop-and-go is the classic industrial controller: {GO at full speed on the path, STOP and wait}. V0 speed-only adds continuous speed modulation but stays pinned to the rail. V0 adds one metre of lateral freedom — the specification's V0 candidate set does include a left and a right shift. V1 replaces V0's nominal proposal with the learned one.

Safety is the shield's job, not the planner's

All three collision-free planners score exactly 0.00, at the same 0.278 m minimum clearance. Cutting the action set down to drive-or-halt does not make the fleet less safe — the space-time shield already guarantees that, at two, three and four workers alike.

Speed modulation carries most of the throughput

Going from drive-or-halt to continuous speed control cuts the stop ratio from 59.0 % to 10.2 % and the makespan from 435 to 361 frames, for 21 ms of extra planning per cluster. Most of what a local replanner is worth here is simply not having to come to a full stop.

The lateral freedom buys smoothness, not much time

One metre of lateral room takes the stop ratio down again, 10.2 % → 3.3 %, but the makespan only 361 → 353 frames. It stops the AMRs waiting; it does not make the mission much shorter.

What V1 buys, in two modes

In safety-first mode the learned action is one more candidate and the shield still picks the cheapest safe one. Safety and completion are untouched and stopping more than halves again (3.30 % → 1.42 %), with 8 % fewer AMR-frames spent under local control. The makespan matches V0's: the win is smoother motion, not a shorter mission.

In proposal-first mode — the specification's V1 flow, where the policy's own action executes whenever it is safe — stopping drops further still, and a good proposal makes the backup set unnecessary: 2.86 candidate rollouts per AMR instead of 8. The cost is 0.4 collisions per run: a small imitation error is unforgiving in a simulator where workers walk into whatever stands in their way.

The specification was right three times

A shortened hard-constraint window, a reduced worker keep-out and a command-consistency cost were all drafted as deviations, to fight an AMR that kept freezing in an aisle. The ablation withdrew all three: the freezing came from a bug in the acceleration limit, and once the manoeuvre kinematics were right, the specified full-horizon constraint won on every metric — 0.00 against 1.00 collisions, 100% against 83.3% completion.

Training

Behaviour cloning against V0's executed command — not its raw proposal, since most of V0's competence lives in the shield's cost ranking — reaches the teacher: zero collisions, every cluster resolved. PPO then briefly passes it (return 35.7 against 35.8, shield overrides 8.5% against 9.6%) and afterwards decays.

With V0 already at zero collisions and full completion, the shield-constrained optimum is essentially attained, so reinforcement learning has little to gain and a great deal to lose. The honest reading is that the benchmark, not the method, is the binding constraint.

Five bugs worth remembering

  1. Per-axis acceleration limits allow a combined |dv| of √2 amax, so the feasibility check rejected every forward candidate whenever a lateral correction was active. Limit the velocity vector.
  2. Lateral offset driven by arc-length cannot move a halted AMR at all, and a smoothstep ramp starts at zero slope, so under 5 Hz re-planning the manoeuvre silently dies. Drive it in time.
  3. Tracked speed stored as a magnitude made every reverse command restart from a forward speed, so the AMR never actually backed off.
  4. Behaviour cloning in raw pre-sigmoid space is ill-conditioned — the teacher saturates at logit ±9. Loss fell 10.3 → 0.007 when moved to decoded action space.
  5. Dropout during PPO makes the importance ratio compare two different networks: sampling runs in eval mode, the update in train mode. Return decayed 36 → 14.

Reproduce

python -m step_e_v1.evaluate --planners stopgo,v0,v1 --seeds 5 --frames 560 \
       --model logs/step_e_v1/v1_best.pt --set v1_prefer_proposal=False \
       --out outputs/8_step_e_v1_module/results/compare_3way.json
python -m step_e_v1.train.ppo_trainer --timesteps 25000 --lr 5e-5 --bc-coef 0.5
python -m step_e_v1.sweep --ablation --seeds 5 --jobs 5
python -m step_e_v1.render --planner v1 --model logs/step_e_v1/v1_best.pt

Everything this package produces is filed under outputs/8_step_e_v1_module/ — metrics in results/, closed-loop videos in demos/ — with checkpoints and the full training log in logs/step_e_v1/. The three earlier Step-E attempts sit in outputs/5_, 6_ and 7_ and are untouched; outputs/README.md says which is which.
